# Bounded worker scheduling verification

Area 7 explores every reachable state and transition of seven finite, source-informed scheduling models, then runs original Odin functions and narrow live server probes. This is bounded verification of the declared models plus implementation tests. It does not prove the executable, weak-memory behavior, or scheduling at arbitrary scale.

Run from the repository root with the installed Python standard library, Odin and clang:

```sh
python3 -I verification/scheduling/verify.py --output "$PWD/verification/scheduling/.build"
```

`--server /absolute/path/to/eski` defaults to `bin/eski`. The server must match the qualified release identity in [binary-identities.json](binary-identities.json), and the complete runtime source inventory must match [source-sha256.json](source-sha256.json). An optional `--tsan-server /absolute/path/to/qualified-eski-tsan` repeats the same socket probes against the qualified instrumented server. The default run always freshly builds and runs the original-source harness with ThreadSanitizer and demonstrates instrumentation with a deliberately racy control. Missing compilers, native link inputs, instrumentation or qualified identities fail the run; they cannot become passing skips. `ODIN` or `ODIN_BIN` selects the installed Odin executable, and `CC` selects clang for recorded tool identity.

The runner clears stale success before any gate. It uses one allowed CPU, nice 10, Odin two compilation threads, a shared verification lock for all real workloads, and the shared compiler lock inside it. Only owned loopback nodes, sockets, temporary directories and subprocesses are created. There are no benchmarks or dependency downloads.

The model has two workers, at most three records and three offered jobs on one to three connections, pool capacity one or three, a per-connection bound of two or three accepted unpublished jobs, at most one internal retry per accepted job, one optional disconnect, and one optional pause/resume cycle. Each connection offers its jobs in request order and connections interleave freely, so one connection can hold several accepted jobs at once. Admission pauses, rather than rejects, while the connection is at its bound or the coordinator holds capacity accepted unpublished jobs, as the original process_input does before parsing. Each offered job is considered once. Rejection consumes the offered request without assigning an accepted sequence; like the coordinator fallback it stands for, it waits until its connection's earlier accepted jobs are published and then delivers its own reply. Abort sets the pool halt flag; affected later jobs may be cancelled internally and replayed once by the coordinator. The coordinator may also execute an offered job itself in one step while no job is accepted and unpublished, nothing is staged or outstanding and no fence or replay is pending: it takes the next accepted position, reads and writes its records and publishes the reply at once, or, when the job aborts, restores the records, sets the halt flag and stages the job for the same fence and ordered retry as a failed worker job. This over-approximates the coordinator fast path in `keyspace_try`, which additionally requires an idle pool or owned records, a constant-time command and no further buffered request, conditions that only narrow when it applies. A client disconnect changes delivery eligibility for every unpublished and later job of that connection while preserving accepted mutation work.

Counts include enabled environment-yield self-loops on nonterminal states. Productive edges advance a finite protocol; the runner checks the complete graph after removing those loops is acyclic. A state with unfinished work and no productive transition is a product deadlock and fails. Infinite yield-only executions represent environment starvation, not a discovered product deadlock. Eventual completion therefore assumes eventual worker and coordinator scheduling, delivery of each modelled signal, broadcast and eventfd write once it is issued, and environment resume. No unbounded liveness or weak-memory result follows.

The operational model uses per-owner FIFO queues and separate worker arrival, mutation, commit/abort, departure, completion, coordinator polling, ordered publication, fence and retry transitions. Multi-owner jobs rendezvous on the distinct owners; duplicate record arguments remain visible to command execution. A job can mutate one argument at a time, making overlap and partial rollback observable. Admission assigns its position in the accepted history only on success. The accepted history is independently interpreted to derive expected per-argument reads, final records and publication order; it is not reconstructed from queue counters.

The model also carries the wakeup protocol. Each worker has a parked flag that it sets under the pool mutex only after finding its owner queue empty; admission clears the flag of every parked owner it enqueues to and signals only those owners. A worker that completes a job into an empty completion ring owes one eventfd write, issued after it releases the mutex, and a completion into a non-empty ring writes nothing. The coordinator sleeps in epoll after taking every completion in one step, wakes when the eventfd is readable or a request arrives, consumes the eventfd only when epoll reported it, and does so before it takes the ring. A pause registers itself under the mutex before it waits, and the worker whose park leaves every worker parked with empty queues broadcasts only while a pause is registered; the fence then collects the ring directly. Three further invariants require that no parked worker has a queued job, that a sleeping coordinator holding completions has a readable eventfd or a write still owed, and that a registered pause on a quiescent pool has a pending broadcast. Two connection invariants require that each connection's replies, including coordinator fallback replies, leave in request order, and that no connection holds more accepted unpublished jobs than its bound nor the coordinator more than the pool capacity. A further invariant requires that a job the coordinator executes itself never starts while any job is accepted and unpublished, staged, outstanding, halted, fencing or awaiting replay; every scenario reaches that transition and the two abort scenarios reach its abort form.

The model's `values` is a single logical record state spanning worker shadows and the reconciled coordinator store. The import fence abstracts the original `keyspace_fence` directory traversal and store import as identity on these logical values. That reconciliation implementation, physical ownership transfer and datastore representation are not proved by this model. The real fixtures exercise selected boundaries separately. Record payloads use a small deterministic update algebra to expose ordering and duplicate arguments; they do not implement every Redis datatype or every command failure mode.

[harness.odin](harness.odin) is a test adapter. The verifier copies the entire original source tree, omits only the application entry point and top-level test files, and adds this adapter. Every retained original file is checked byte for byte and inventoried before compiling. Original pool, coordinator, snapshot and command functions execute unchanged. The coordinator fixture injects bounded completion records to make missing-head and retry order deterministic; its ordering adapter then calls original INCR, and original GET independently observes each result. A second injection gives one live connection three in-flight jobs, completes them out of order and requires their replies in accepted order with a single resume ticket. This fixture is separate from the socket probes. The snapshot fixture is a real allocation-budget failure with original rollback. The pool fixture's abort callback restores its own temporary clock write before signalling abort; it is not presented as a replacement for the separately tested snapshot function.

Each fresh normal and ThreadSanitizer harness run requires all 21 named checks: ten pool checks, seven coordinator checks and four snapshot checks. The deliberate [race control](race_control.odin) must exit 66 with an actual ThreadSanitizer report naming the shared test variable; the production harness must then exit zero without a report under the same toolchain and sanitizer settings. There are no suppressions. The default release server runs ten targeted checks, with at least twelve observed worker admissions. Because a lone string command with nothing else buffered now runs on the coordinator, the probe pipelines the failing SET and each disconnecting INCR with a following GET so that they still reach the workers. The optional qualified ThreadSanitizer server repeats them. Live socket disconnect tests establish accepted worker writes and final records for four owned connections; they do not enumerate all operating-system socket schedules. These checks establish no blanket race-freedom claim.

The explorer emits complete compressed state/edge graphs, exact terminal inventories, transition coverage and hashes. A second relation audit checks every saved state's enabled successors, exact edge equality and reachability from the root. State caps and deadlines fail rather than produce `exhausted: true`. A separately checkable four-bit exploration control has exactly 16 states, 32 edges and one terminal, and its full edge set is compared against an independent integer enumeration. A deliberately too-small exploration cap must fail.

The same explorer and invariants must reject ten real transition mutants: bypassed multi-owner ownership, out-of-order publication, dropping an accepted disconnected write, a worker that marks itself parked after releasing the mutex, a coordinator that takes completions before consuming the eventfd, a pause that registers its wait after checking quiescence, a coordinator fallback that replies before its connection's earlier accepted jobs are published, admission past the per-connection bound, admission past the pool capacity, and a coordinator that executes a job itself while another accepted job is unpublished. Each counterexample includes every state and transition from the initial state, its violated invariant, scenario and mutant name. Every normal verification run replays all witnesses through the same operational relation. To replay one preserved witness independently:

```sh
python3 -I verification/scheduling/verify.py --replay /absolute/path/to/bypass_ownership-witness.json
```

The final receipt records model bounds, exact explored counts, invariant and transition coverage, graph audits, rejected mutants, witness hashes, actual check results, compiler versions, native input hashes, executable identities, the complete runtime source inventory and verification asset hashes. Generated files stay in the selected output directory. The declared finite scenarios, tests and explicit environmental assumptions are the scope of the result.
